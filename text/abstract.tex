Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) is a sample-based planning framework for sequential decision-making problems, which has produced state-of-the-art results across many domains. While most algorithms underpinning these results are designed for a single objective, \mccorrect{many real-world problems involve trade-offs between conflicting objectives, whose relative weighting is unknown at planning time}. This thesis first improves exploration in single-objective MCTS, and then extends those improvements to the multi-objective setting.

In the single-objective setting, the maximum entropy objective function, which is commonly used to encourage exploration, is shown to not always align with \mccorrect{the standard objective of maximising expected return}, both theoretically and empirically. Boltzmann Tree Search (BTS) is adapted from Maximum ENtropy Tree Search (MENTS) by removing entropy from the objective function. Decaying ENtropy Tree Search (DENTS) then re-introduces entropy as an exploration bonus during planning, \mccorrect{while still recommending actions that maximise expected return}. BTS and DENTS are shown to converge under mild conditions, and outperform Upper Confidence Bound applied to Trees (UCT) and MENTS on gridworld environments and on the game of Go.

In the multi-objective setting, two Multi-Objective MCTS (MOMCTS) frameworks are introduced for the \mccorrect{unknown weights scenario with linear utilities}. The first is Convex Hull MCTS (CHMCTS), which generalises prior MOMCTS work to stochastic environments via convex hull backups, but at high computational cost. The second is the simplex map, an adaptive data structure that uses a hierarchical triangulation of the unit simplex to maintain value and entropy estimates as a function of how the objectives are weighted. Simplex maps integrate cleanly with the entropy bonus used by DENTS, whereas convex hull backups do not. The methods developed here outperform existing MOMCTS methods on multi-objective gridworld and MO-Gymnasium environments, with simplex maps scaling substantially better than CHMCTS in stochastic environments.
