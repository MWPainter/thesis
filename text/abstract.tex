Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) is a sample-based planning framework for sequential decision-making problems, which has produced state-of-the-art results \mccorrect{across many} domains. \mccorrect{While} most algorithms underpinning these results are designed for a single objective, many real-world problems involve trade-offs between \mccorrect{conflicting objectives, whose relative weighting is unknown at planning time}. \mccorrect{This thesis first improves exploration in single-objective MCTS, and then extends those improvements to the multi-objective setting.}

\mccorrect{In the single-objective setting,} the maximum entropy objective, \mccorrect{which is commonly used to encourage exploration, is shown to not always align} with the \mccorrect{standard objective of maximising expected return}, both \mccorrect{theoretically} and empirically. Boltzmann Tree Search (BTS) is adapted from Maximum ENtropy Tree Search (MENTS) by removing entropy from the objective. Decaying ENtropy Tree Search (DENTS) then re-introduces entropy as a \mccorrect{second objective during planning, while still recommending actions that maximise expected return}. BTS and DENTS are shown to \mccorrect{converge} under mild conditions, and outperform Upper Confidence Bound \mccorrect{applied to Trees (UCT)} and MENTS on gridworld environments and on the game of Go.

In the multi-objective setting, two Multi-Objective MCTS (MOMCTS) frameworks are introduced for the \mccorrect{unknown weights scenario} with linear utilities. The first is Convex Hull MCTS (CHMCTS), which generalises prior MOMCTS work to stochastic environments via convex hull backups, \mccorrect{but at high computational cost}. The second is the simplex map, an adaptive data structure that \mccorrect{uses a hierarchical triangulation of the unit simplex to maintain} value and entropy estimates as a function of \mccorrect{how the objectives are weighted}. Simplex maps integrate cleanly with the entropy bonus used by DENTS, \mccorrect{whereas convex hull backups do not}. The methods developed in this thesis outperform existing MOMCTS methods on multi-objective gridworld \mccorrect{and MO-Gymnasium environments}, with simplex map algorithms scaling \mccorrect{substantially} better than CHMCTS in stochastic environments.
